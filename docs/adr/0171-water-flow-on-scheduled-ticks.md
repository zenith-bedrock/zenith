# ADR §171 — Liquids flow on scheduled ticks

> Part of the [decision log](README.md). Code and docs cite this record as `ADR §171`.

**Context:** Step 3 of `plans/world-dynamics-2026-11-15.md`. Water existed only as sea-level source blocks, and `Blocks.IsFluid` knew a single runtime id (depth 0), so a flowing level would not have counted as fluid at all. §164 already gave the server a block-update queue and `ScheduledTicks`.

**Choice:**
- Water is the `minecraft:water` family with Bedrock's `liquid_depth`: 0 source, 1–7 flowing thinner, +8 falling. New `IntKey` and `StateKeys.LiquidDepth`; `BlockStateTable.TryGet/TrySet` for it.
- "Is a fluid" is data: the `Water` and `Lava` traits on `minecraft:water` and `minecraft:lava` in `block_definitions.json` (kind stays `Unsupported`, so neither is placeable, breakable or an item). `Blocks.FluidOf(id)` answers `FluidType.None/Water/Lava` for every depth; `Blocks.FluidIds` is gone.
- `FluidFlow` (one class, parametrised by a `FluidSpec`) follows the vanilla fluid algorithm; water is delay 5, slope reach 4, drop-off 1, infinite sources, lava is delay 30, reach 2, drop-off 2, no infinite sources. When a neighbour changes, `BlockUpdateSystem` schedules a water cell five ticks out; when due, `WaterFlow.Tick` runs. A flowing cell first re-derives its level (two sources beside it over solid ground or a source: a source; water above: falling; else the strongest side neighbour minus one; nothing: it dries up). The sideways search is one breadth-first walk from all four neighbours (about 30 µs per evaluation, down from about 57 µs with a depth-first walk per side). Then it spreads: into open space below (sideways too only when three sources touch it), otherwise sideways, and sideways only towards the nearest drop-off found by a slope search of up to four blocks (all four ways when none is in reach). Spreading only fills cells that are not water; existing water re-derives itself when woken, so a source is never overwritten. Falling water spreads at level 1.
- Open space is air, or a plant or torch, which a liquid destroys and drops like any world-caused break (`BlockSpill`, shared with the unsupported-block break). Doors and signs are not washed away: they do not hold a liquid, they block it. (The earlier note that vanilla water destroys doors was wrong.)
- Lava and water: a lava cell that finds water above or beside it turns into obsidian when it is a source and cobblestone when it is flowing; lava falling onto water makes stone (`FluidFlow.TryReact`, run whenever a lava cell is evaluated). A chunk that is not resident is never read as open and never loaded.
- Buckets: `BucketItems` resolves the empty, water, lava and powder-snow buckets as data (`FilledBucket`); the use handler routes any bucket click to a `BucketIntent` and `BucketSystem` (after block edits) applies it: an empty bucket takes a liquid source or a powder-snow block (the clicked cell or the one the click faced), a filled bucket pours into the clicked cell if it can take it, else the faced one. Reach and the held stack are re-checked in the tick; creative keeps its bucket; a stack of empty buckets fills one and needs inventory room for the full one. A refused use resyncs both cells to the player. Milk is drunk through the food pipeline (`FoodDefinition.ClearsEffects`, remainder an empty bucket) and an empty bucket used on a cow turns into a milk bucket (`CowSystem.TryMilk`).
- Bodies in liquid. Players move client-side, so buoyancy and the stroke are the client's; the server judges consequences in `AirSupplySystem`: head under water runs the air supply down from 300, at -20 the player takes 2 damage (armour does not reduce it) and the count restarts, surfacing refills 4 per tick, feet in lava take 4 damage whenever the damage window allows, creative is untouched, a fall into liquid resets the fall peak, and sprinting with feet and head in water sets the swimming pose flag for peers. Mobs: `GroundMobMovement` makes them rise to the surface of a liquid and not take fall damage, and `MobHazardSystem` applies lava damage (4 every 10 ticks) and the same breath rule through `DamageDispatch`, so each species keeps its death consequences; fish are exempt.

**Why:** No new system: the cost model, bounds (`MaxScheduledPerTick`, the queue and heap caps) and the single-writer rule of §164 carry over. A still lake costs nothing, since nothing wakes it until something next to it changes.

**Invariants:** A source on flat ground reaches seven blocks with levels 1..7, or runs only towards a drop-off within four blocks; falling water runs down shafts; removing the source dries the flow; the infinite-source rule holds; a plant is washed away; a source is never overwritten; flow stops at the loaded edge without loading (`WaterFlowTests`). Buckets obey reach, held-stack and creative rules (`BucketTests`).

**Non-goals / Deferred:** Fish buckets (only one fish species exists, with no variants, and capturing a fish needs an entity-interact path that does not exist), powder snow as a block (sinking, freezing, the bucket only pours and scoops it), bucket and flow sounds, `flowing_water` as a distinct block, waterlogging, lava fire (done in §172), mobs avoiding or fleeing liquid, suffocation of fish out of water, and a wire smoke of any of this against a real client.

**Verification:** Unit tests: flow, lava and buckets (`WaterFlowTests`, `BucketTests`), body physics (`FluidPhysicsTests`, 11), milk (`HungerSystemTests`, `CowSystemTests`). Flood cost (`FluidFloodCostTests`: 36 sources over 3x3 chunks, 400 ticks, warm): p99 about 9 to 19 ms, worst tick up to 31 ms, queues stay under their soft caps; one run under machine load saw a 71 ms spike. Not verified on a real client: the swimming flag index (56), the air-supply metadata keys (7 and 42), and the Bedrock rendering of flowing depth values (assumed from the state names).
